Learn how to turn a vague software request into requirements you can check. In Lecture 2 of the 14-part Software Engineering course, we follow the fictional Campus Rooms service from “make room booking fair and easy” to explicit rules, boundary examples, quality conditions and traceable decisions. The central question: how would we know that a requirement has been satisfied?

You will learn how to ask stakeholders for concrete examples, separate facts from assumptions and open questions, define accepted and rejected behavior, and test the edges of a 30–120 minute booking rule. We compare vague and precise requests to an assistant, examine why an average response time hides slow users, and connect requirements to design decisions, examples and checks.

Main topics include requirements engineering, elicitation and scope, behavior and quality requirements, business rules, acceptance examples, boundary testing, response-time percentiles, keyboard accessibility, verification versus validation, traceability, prioritization and change management.

Use the chapters to jump to any demonstration or revisit the requirement register and closing checks.
