This is the fourth lecture of the Software Engineering course. A small team builds Campus Rooms, a fictional room-booking service, with eight work cards on one board: after four rounds every station has been busy, yet only three cards are done and five wait for review. Eight interactive scenes answer one question: where is work waiting, and what would actually improve the flow?

You will learn why finished parts can still be one late discovery, how to find a bottleneck from queues instead of from how busy people look, why a work-in-progress limit shortens cycle time while lead time stays the same, why a range with its assumptions beats a single number, which next step reduces the most uncertainty, what separates coded from ready to rely on, and how to test a process change like a hypothesis.

Main topics include feedback batches and slices, increments and iterations, plan-driven work, Scrum and Kanban, bottlenecks, work in progress, throughput, work item age, cycle time and lead time, estimates and ranges, risk, reversibility, cost of delay, the Definition of Done, blameless retrospectives and sources.
