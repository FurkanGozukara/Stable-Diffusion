How to read the comparison: each round restarts the same source passage and preserves the videos' natural speed. The shared audio is played once, rather than mixing six soundtracks. A progress line tracks the passage. The full-result view preserves each source's aspect ratio; the face detail is a simultaneous crop from that same video. Labels identify the model, recipe and restoration pass.

The 38 outputs include settings and restoration variants, not 38 different base models. Seven rounds use the complete ten-second passage. The final round plays the complete first sentence alongside short HuMo, restrained-prompt LongCat and higher-resolution Wan clips, plus AvatarForever. Short video tails use labelled final-frame holds so the complete spoken passage remains audible.

Test scope: one portrait and a neutral passage, with different native resolutions, frame rates and generation settings. Judge identity, movement and mouth detail together. A sharper face does not by itself prove better lip sync. This video does not establish a universal model ranking, an emotional-performance benchmark, a hardware-speed comparison or a validated continuous lip-sync score. Visible artifacts remain in the tested results, including the ghosting in the two-reference H3 example, so you can inspect the tradeoffs yourself.

Use the chapters to compare model families, generation settings and mouth-restoration variants. The final chapter also includes the short first-sentence tests.
